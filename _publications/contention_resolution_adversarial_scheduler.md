We study contention resolution when an adversarial scheduler chooses which players may participate at each step. With a shared clock and shared randomness, players can achieve optimal $$\Theta(1/\log n)$$ efficiency even against an adaptive scheduler. Without either form of shared state, efficiency is at most $$O(\log n/n)$$.
