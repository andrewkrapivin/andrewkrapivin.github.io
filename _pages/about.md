Welcome to my site! I'm Andrew Krapivin, a second-year PhD student in computer science at CMU, advised by [William Kuszmaul](https://sites.google.com/site/williamkuszmaul) and [Elaine Shi](https://elaineshi.com/). Broadly, I am interested in developing theory, particularly in data structures, and finding new applications in which it can be useful. My recent work spans space-efficient hashing, oblivious data structures, and SAT solving: open-addressed hash tables that beat long-standing bounds (the first paper in this series was [featured in Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/undergraduate-upends-a-40-year-old-data-science-conjecture-20250210/)), optimal statistically secure ORAM and oblivious search trees, compact SAT encodings, and optimal hypergraph (biclique) decompositions. I also created PointSAT and used it to resolve an open problem in discrete geometry. Beyond that, I have worked on a fast and practical filter implementation, on a computational model to capture out of order execution on modern CPUs, on comparing encoders and (reasoning) decoders from a theoretical perspective, and more.

I previously completed an MPhil in Advanced Computer Science at Cambridge University, funded by a [Churchill Scholarship](https://www.churchillscholarship.org/). Before that, I received a BS in mathematics and computer science from Rutgers University.

If you want to discuss anything, feel free to reach out!
